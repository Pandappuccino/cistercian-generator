What are Cistercian Numerals?

Cistercian numerals are a form of numeric ligature developed in the thirteenth century by the Cistercian monastic order as a way of writing numerals using a singular glyph. Originally, it was a two-digit glyph that went from 1 to 99, introduced to the order by archdeacon John of Basingstoke, and was based on ars notoria, a form of shorthand used in the twelfth century. It was subsequently expanded to represent the numbers 1 to 9999. The traditional numbers were written using a horizontal "stave", but the vertical stave was used in Northern France until the fourteenth century. When Cistercian numerals made a comeback in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries in France and Germany, they exclusively used the vertical stave.

Each glyph consists of one central line (the stave), and up to four additional glyphs; the placement of each glyph denotes the placement of the number (ones, tens, hundreds, or thousands), and the glyph itself is the digit. For example, this is the number 3851 as a Cistercian number:

In vertical-stave glyphs, the number is read from left to right and from bottom to top. In order, the glyph is read from lower-left, lower-right, upper-left, and upper-right.

Horizontal glyphs are subsequently read from bottom to top and from right to left: lower-right, upper-right, lower-left, upper-left. Note that there is no glyph in the tens position; 0 is not represented with any sort of glyph, and if a number has a 0 in it, the placement is left blank.
